Human activities are transforming ecosystems, but what does that mean for the microorganisms living in and on wildlife? And more importantly, what are the consequences for the animals themselves? In this *Unsolved Mystery* article, we explore three major, interacting pathways through which humans reshape wildlife microbiomes: **pollution, habitat transformation, and altered host contact networks**. These pressures can disrupt long-standing host–microbe relationships, favor the spread of antimicrobial resistance, and create new opportunities for microbial exchange between humans, domestic animals, and wildlife.

Although evidence that human activities alter wildlife microbiomes is accumulating, we still know surprisingly little about the mechanisms driving these changes or their consequences. Why do similar disturbances produce different microbial responses across host species? Are these changes temporary or persistent, harmful or adaptive? And when do changes in microbial communities actually affect wildlife health?

We highlight some of the most important unanswered questions and outline research approaches, including longitudinal field studies, strain-resolved metagenomics, and targeted experiments, that can help move the field beyond describing microbiome changes toward understanding their causes and consequences. Ultimately, this framework connects many of the questions motivating my own research and underscores why understanding wildlife microbiomes is increasingly important for conservation and One Health.
